# Editorial profile 1

These are original operational checks derived from the author's editing feedback. They are not a copy of, or a version claim about, upstream Humanizer.

## Compatibility and fallback

Use an installed Humanizer when available. Consider it compatible only if it covers factual preservation, sample-based voice matching, and the additional checks below. Record the selected skill path/version and missing capabilities briefly in working notes. Do not demand a particular version string or trust an editorial-profile marker from an unrelated package.

If compatible, avoid duplicating instructions. If incomplete, combine it with the missing checks here before rewriting. If absent, this document is the complete minimum editing fallback. Users need no separate installation.

## Baseline

Preserve substantive claims, qualifiers, numbers, quotations, names, and source relationships. Verify new factual additions separately; do not invent incidents or make a hypothetical example sound autobiographical. Never add detail merely to sound human.

Match a supplied sample's vocabulary, sentence rhythm, transitions, and deliberate quirks. Explicit user corrections take priority. Without a sample, use direct explanatory prose suited to the audience; do not manufacture first-person experience, slang, humor, or emotion.

Remove filler, inflated importance, promotional language, unnecessary hedging, vague authority claims, and mechanical repetition. Keep uncertainty that changes the meaning. Use clear verbs and concrete explanations; preserve technical terms when they are useful.

## Additional checks

- Empty bridges: “Camus was describing something more specific” announces a distinction without explaining it. State the distinction already supported by the text or remove the bridge. “This is where…” is not automatically wrong.
- Vague abstractions: ask what “a choice carries real weight” means in context. Explain the actual consequence if known; otherwise simplify or cut. Replacing one vague metaphor with another is not a fix.
- Repeated structure: inspect adjacent paragraphs for fact → dramatic supporting fact → third fact → takeaway. Develop the examples that add information and vary structure naturally.
- Manufactured emphasis: isolated lines such as “We still had to choose” or “That is enough to move” can repeat surrounding claims. Merge or remove them when their only function is drama.
- Corrective formulas: inspect repeated “not X, but Y” and “This does not mean X. It means Y.” Preserve useful distinctions and real objections; remove invented contrasts.
- Forced completeness: three examples, a three-step framework, or a recap should earn their space. Do not delete a meaningful item merely to avoid a count of three.
- Empty credibility signals: a sentence naming a prestigious encyclopedia without explaining anything may interrupt an essay. Retain substantive attribution and disputed-claim sourcing. Optional reading may move to an end references section while preserving existing URLs and clear claim-source relationships.
- Unnecessary judgment: “adolescent” adds a judgment that “nihilistic” does not. They are not synonyms; choose wording that preserves the intended claim and user corrections.

## Final read

Read the entire article, including opening and ending. Does every paragraph develop the thought? Are transitions explaining relationships? Are factual qualifications intact? Did compression remove needed substance? Does the draft reproduce a pattern the user explicitly rejected?

Short paragraphs, metaphors, contrasts, citations, and polished prose are not proof of AI authorship. Never use zero regex matches or a self-awarded score as proof that the voice is right.
