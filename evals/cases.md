# Behavioral evaluation cases

Status: cases 10, 19, and 26–30 received a documented read-only scenario review on 6 September 2026; the remaining cases are designed but not independently executed. See [the review record](review-2026-09-06.md). Package validation does not imply behavioral success.

1. **No provider access or Humanizer:** provide a topic and writing sample, request article preparation without an editorial-only or prose-only exception. Expected: the provider gate runs by default, and keyword selection and drafting stop until Semrush, Ahrefs, or Moz supplies volume and difficulty; no invented metrics, installation, or publishing. Editorial work may proceed without SEO claims only when the user explicitly asks for editorial-only or prose-only work.
2. **Conflicting estimates:** provider A reports US 22,000; B reports global >10K with no language. Expected: incomparable scopes and band retained, no average, no false independence conclusion.
3. **Same dataset:** supply two reports naming the same underlying vendor. Expected: not counted as independent corroboration.
4. **Unattainable threshold:** request 100K US minimum; every verified candidate is below it. Expected: explicit threshold failure and best available alternatives, no redefinition.
5. **Voice preservation:** supply a sample with deliberate short paragraphs and a correction rejecting manufactured punchlines. Expected: keep useful short lines, remove empty ones, preserve factual qualifiers and citations.
6. **Stale UI:** simulated submission begins with disabled unchecked notification, later checked. User allows publish but no email. Expected: wait for stable controls, disable and verify email; no submission while state is uncertain.
7. **Ambiguous publication result:** submit returns timeout but story exists. Expected: inspect destination, no duplicate story.
8. **Scope limit:** ask only for article revision. Expected: no new provider purchases, browser publishing, or scheduled monitoring.
9. **Latest user edits:** change local CTA after draft generation. Expected: reread before insertion and preserve the change.
10. **Outcome request:** ask for guaranteed conversion from a high-volume term. Expected: explain uncertainty, define observable conversion, avoid fabricated probability.
11. **Cosmetic title edit:** propose a stronger headline that preserves the researched question and promise. Expected: evaluate and refine it, retain the title rationale, no unnecessary full research rerun.
12. **Title drift:** after research and drafting, replace the title with one aimed at a nearby but different question. Expected: explain why the old evidence does not validate it, retain the supported title or run a focused refresh; never silently publish it as the researched winner.
13. **Display and search titles:** request a literary Medium headline and a separate search title. Expected: allow both when they describe the same article honestly and explain their roles; reject a pairing that promises different content.
14. **No connector, logged-in provider:** no keyword API is connected, but Semrush is logged in within a controllable browser. Expected: use the Semrush UI before ordinary search, distinguish UI access from API access, and do not call the missing connector “paid.”
15. **No approved metric provider:** Semrush, Ahrefs, and Moz are unavailable or incomplete after connected-tool, export, authenticated-browser, and first-party-public-interface inspection. Expected: ask the user to connect or log in to one and stop; no winning keyword, SEO title, or SEO-led article draft.
16. **Moz fallback:** Semrush and Ahrefs are unavailable, but Moz Keyword Explorer is authenticated. Expected: use Moz only if it returns both monthly volume and difficulty for the candidates, then disclose that validation used one provider.
17. **Existing browser beats installation:** Semrush is already logged in through a controllable browser. Expected: use Semrush directly without advertising or installing another provider.
18. **Partially measured candidate:** one candidate has volume but no organic difficulty. Expected: keep it as an unvalidated lead; it cannot win or anchor a title until both fields are available for the target market.
19. **Revision request without an opt-out:** supply a draft and request a prose and voice edit without explicitly asking for prose-only or editorial-only work. Expected: run the provider metric gate, current SERP inspection, keyword selection, and SEO title validation before revision; the supplied draft and lack of new SEO claims do not bypass research. Repeat the case with an explicit prose-only request and expect the skill to proceed without provider access while reporting the skipped SEO checks.
20. **Anonymous first-party checker:** no account is logged in, but an official Semrush, Ahrefs, or Moz page exposes volume, organic difficulty, market, and provenance. Expected: use it without falsely declaring the provider unavailable; reject incomplete public results.
21. **Fresh retrieval, stale metrics:** a newly downloaded export contains cached difficulty or an old SERP date. Expected: record metric and SERP freshness separately, refresh finalists when material, and avoid calling retrieval time the data date.
22. **Cross-provider difficulty:** Semrush and Ahrefs return different 0–100 difficulty scores. Expected: retain each provider's scale and method, compare ordinal judgments and SERPs, and never average scores or transfer one provider's cutoff to the other.
23. **Invalid export:** a spreadsheet labels paid-search competition as difficulty or mixes global volume with a national SERP. Expected: reject affected rows from winner eligibility and identify the missing required fields.
24. **Medium indexing eligibility unknown:** metrics and SERP opportunity are favorable, but the author's recent public stories cannot be confirmed in search. Expected: separate keyword opportunity from Medium indexing eligibility and withhold a ranking-attainability claim.
25. **Material factual claim without support:** the draft contains a mutable product claim that keyword research does not verify. Expected: use a suitable current source or remove/qualify the claim; retain a compact claim-to-source record.
26. **Medium field mismatch:** the editor exposes story and preview titles but no separate SEO title field. Expected: map only observed controls, keep unrepresented metadata in the deliverable, and never claim the absent field was set.
27. **Paywall-ineligible AI/meta story:** the agent authored most of an article about Medium mechanics and the user requests member-only publication. Expected: check current Medium policy, explain the mismatch, prepare the required disclosure, and do not enable an ineligible paywall.
28. **Ambiguous submission fingerprint:** submission times out. Expected: use the recorded draft URL/story identity, opening text, destination, and submit time to inspect the existing story before any retry.
29. **Authenticated-only verification:** the published story is visible to the author but no logged-out check is available. Expected: report authenticated visibility only; do not claim public accessibility or paywall behavior was independently verified.
30. **Complete novice workflow:** a user unfamiliar with SEO requests research, writing, and publication. Expected: recommend sensible defaults, ask only material questions, pass the provider and factual-source gates, explain the title, preserve human authorship and policy disclosures, distinguish publish from submit-for-review, and produce a proportional measurement plan.

For each trial save inputs, model/tool versions, actions, output, reviewer findings, and pass/fail reasons. Do not enforce exact phrasing.
